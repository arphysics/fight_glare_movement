Fight Glare — heading font test

Same real content, five heading fonts. Body copy is Lexend in every version — that part is settled. The question here is the heading font (--font-head), which a reviewer said reads too futuristic as Roboto Mono. Note that on the live site --font-head also drives the small uppercase tags (“Irvine & Orange County, CA”, “The Problem”), so those swap with it here too. Each panel ends with the exact line to paste into :root in index.html.

Warmest of the four — soft, slightly hand-cut old-style serif that echoes lamplight rather than LED, and gives the headline a human voice without looking old-fashioned. Its large x-height sits level with Lexend.

Caveat: Fraunces is a display face with extra axes (optical size, SOFT, WONK). Google Fonts serves sensible defaults for a plain wght request, but it has real personality — check the long section title above, not just the H1. It's also the largest download of the four.
--font-head: 'Fraunces', Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif;

The most institutional of the four — this is the register of a staff report or a public comment letter, which is exactly the room this site is arguing in. Trustworthy first, characterful second.

Caveat: transitional serifs have thin strokes, and thin light-on-dark strokes shrink optically against navy. Use weight 600–700 for headings, never 400. It's also the most reserved option — it can read a little dry next to an H1 that ends in an exclamation mark.
--font-head: 'Source Serif 4', Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif;

The smallest jump from where the site is now: Bitter keeps the sturdy, squared-off, built-infrastructure feel of the mono but reads as printed matter rather than a terminal. Sturdy strokes hold up well as light text on dark navy.

Caveat: slabs carry a lot of visual weight — the long section title above is the real test, and it may need its size dropped a notch. Bitter's x-height is large like Lexend's, so headings and body can start to look like the same size; keep a clear step between them.
--font-head: 'Bitter', Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif;

Keeps the page all-sans and un-fussy, but its flared, pen-drawn letterforms are warm where Lexend is neutral — human without switching the site into serif territory at all.

Caveat: two things to watch. (1) Alegreya Sans has no weight 600 on Google Fonts — index.html asks for 600 on h1/h2/h3, so the browser silently renders 700 instead; set the weight explicitly rather than leaving it to chance. (2) Its x-height is noticeably smaller than Lexend's, so a heading looks smaller than its size suggests — headings likely need a size bump to keep the step above body copy.
--font-head: 'Alegreya Sans', system-ui, sans-serif;

Loading cost, for all four: the site already fetches two families (Roboto Mono + Lexend). Swapping --font-head replaces Roboto Mono rather than adding to it, so the request count doesn't change. It only grows if you keep Roboto Mono around for the small uppercase tags — that's a defensible choice, since mono suits tracked micro-labels, but it's a third family on the wire.
