Irvine, let's fight glare!

Irvine, CA, in replacing its streetlights with LEDs, is trading quiet evening ambience for loud illuminance. Across the city, residents report these newer fixtures as harsh and painful to the eye. Currently, the city's code offers no ceiling on how bright a light may be and no limit on its unnatural, piercing blue. We are a group of concerned residents asking Irvine to establish a standard and restore the night to our streets.

The Problem

Irvine's new streetlights are too bright, too blue, and too exposed, which leads to

👁️

Visual stress

The harsh glare and icy blue-white light weary the eyes, fracturing the streets into uneven pools of light and dark. Rather than guiding us, this unnatural contrast obscures the path, making it harder rather than easier to navigate the night.

😴

Sleep and health

This stark, blue-rich light disrupts our natural circadian rhythms and suppresses melatonin, while unshielded fixtures bleed this glare directly into the rooms where we sleep. For residents living with migraines, autism, or photophobia, this relentless illumination takes an even heavier toll.

🏠

Light spillage

Unshielded fixtures spill into the sky and into bedrooms and yards, causing unwelcome nuisance in homes and washing out the night sky.

The Goal

Irvine's first lighting ordinance

Irvine's current code promises that outdoor lighting will be contained and neighbors protected from the glare — yet these protections are hollow, offering no way to measure the intrusion and setting absolutely no limit on the harsh color temperature. We seek a city-wide standard anchored in firm, enforceable numbers, demanding illumination that is

  • Well-shielded: Directed strictly downward where it belongs, never allowed to pierce the eyes, bleed into our windows, or erase the night sky.
  • Warm in tone: Capped at 2700K to restore a gentler glow, with a softer amber preferred near our parks, quiet trails, and open spaces.
  • Right-sized: Emitting not a fraction more brightness than the task demands, dimming or yielding to the dark when pathways are empty.
  • Considerate: Deeply mindful of our shared spaces, offering a quiet reprieve rather than a harsh assault on neighbors and light-sensitive passersby.

Responsible lighting asks that you

Reduce

Use only as much light as the task needs; this includes picking the lowest possible color temperature (amber tones wherever possible, and no more than 2700K outdoors at night).

Restrict

Use timers, dimmers, and sensors so light runs only when and where it's actually needed.

Redirect

Shield fixtures to aim light down at the ground, not into eyes, windows, and the sky.

The Facts

What we ask for is no radical departure but a necessary restraint that other cities have already recognized and woven into the fabric of their own codes.

The American Medical Association itself urges that community streetlamps be shielded and capped at 3000K to tame the piercing glare and protect the quiet sanctity of our sleep. We are asking Irvine to go further, demanding a gentler limit of 2700K or below. (AMA, 2016)

Even California's own building codes point the same way: CALGreen's voluntary tier measures cap general hardscape lighting at 3000K, while the state energy code curbs the backlight, uplight, and glare that spill from large outdoor fixtures. Both, however, carve out public street lighting — and that omission is precisely the gap a city standard exists to fill. The state has already turned back toward a warmer dusk; we ask Irvine to finish the turn.

We want Irvine to join a growing list of dark-sky-friendly communities which include Malibu, CA; Tucson, AZ; Flagstaff, AZ; Chesterfield, MO.

Two questions people ask

"But isn't brighter safer?"

We do not ask Irvine to plunge our streets into darkness, for we recognize that illumination guards our roads — the true question is how we bind and guide this light. The harsh glare of an unshielded fixture does not reveal the path; instead, it scatters sharply within the eye, washing out contrast and blinding us to what lies ahead. Fracturing the streets into pools of stark radiance against chasms of sudden black makes the shadows deeper and harder to pierce than a steady, even glow. True safety is found not in a merciless blaze, but in the careful, quiet quality of the light.

"Won't this be expensive?"

Not in any true measure. A warmer hue and a well-shielded fixture are not luxuries bought at a premium — the color of the light is simply a choice made at the time of order. Though a softer, warmer glow may draw a breath more power, this slight difference fades into shadow when weighed against the vast savings these new lights already deliver over the old lamps they replace.

Dear Irvine City Council,

I am an Irvine resident writing to express my deep concern regarding the harsh glare and piercing brightness of the city's new LED streetlights. I am asking the Council to establish an outdoor lighting standard that requires a warmer tone (2700K or below), proper shielding, and sensible dimming — the very restraints the American Medical Association recommends.

While this is not a demand for darkness, it is a request that the illumination we pay for is cast downward onto the pavement, rather than into our eyes and the quiet of our bedrooms or yards.

Thank you for your time and your attention to this matter.

[Your name], [Your neighborhood]
