Irvine, let's fight glare!

Irvine, CA, in replacing its streetlights with LEDs, is trading quiet evening ambience for loud illuminance. Across the city, residents report these newer fixtures as harsh and painful to the eye. Currently, the city's code offers no ceiling on how bright a light may be and no limit on its unnatural, piercing blue. We are a group of concerned residents asking Irvine to establish a standard and restore the night to our streets and accessibility of public spaces to all our residents.

The Problem

Irvine's new streetlights are too bright, too blue, and too exposed, which leads to

👁️

Visual stress

The harsh glare and icy blue-white light weary the eyes, fracturing the streets into uneven pools of light and dark. Rather than guiding us, this unnatural contrast obscures the path, making it harder rather than easier to navigate the night.

😴

Compromised sleep and health

This stark, blue-rich light disrupts our natural circadian rhythms and suppresses melatonin, while unshielded fixtures bleed this glare directly into the rooms where we sleep. For residents living with migraines, autism, or photophobia, this relentless illumination takes an even heavier toll.

🏠

Light spillage

Unshielded fixtures spill into the sky and into bedrooms and yards, causing unwelcome nuisance in homes and washing out the night sky.

The Goal

Irvine's first lighting ordinance

Irvine's current code promises that outdoor lighting will be contained and neighbors protected from the glare. Yet these protections are hollow, offering no way to measure the intrusion and setting absolutely no limit on the harsh color temperature. We seek a city-wide standard anchored in firm, enforceable numbers, demanding illumination that is

  • Well-shielded: Directed strictly downward where it belongs, never allowed to pierce the eyes, bleed into our windows, or erase the night sky.
  • Warm in tone: Capped at 2700K to restore a gentler glow, with a softer amber preferred near our parks, quiet trails, and open spaces.
  • Right-sized: Emitting not a fraction more brightness than the task demands, dimming or yielding to the dark when pathways are empty.
  • Considerate: Deeply mindful of our shared spaces, offering a quiet reprieve rather than a harsh assault on neighbors and light-sensitive passersby.

Responsible lighting asks that you

Reduce

Use only as much light as the task needs; this includes picking the lowest possible color temperature (amber tones wherever possible, and no more than 2700K outdoors at night).

Restrict

Use timers, dimmers, and sensors so light runs only when and where it's actually needed.

Redirect

Shield fixtures to aim light down at the ground, not into eyes, windows, and the sky.

The Facts

What we ask for is no radical departure but a necessary restraint that other cities have already recognized and woven into the fabric of their own codes.

The American Medical Association calls for community streetlamps to be shielded and capped at 3000K or below. We urge Irvine to go further, to a gentler 2700K. (AMA, 2016)

CALGreen's voluntary tier holds general hardscape lighting to 3000K, and the state energy code reins in the backlight, uplight, and glare that spill from large outdoor fixtures. But public street lighting falls outside the reach of both, an omission only a city standard can correct. Irvine must hold every streetlight to this standard.

We want Irvine to join a growing list of dark-sky-friendly communities which include Malibu, CA; Tucson, AZ; Flagstaff, AZ; Chesterfield, MO.

Two questions people ask

"But isn't brighter safer?"

We recognize that our streets should not be plunged into darkness. The question is how much light our roads actually need, and where that light should be aimed. An unshielded fixture scatters light inside the eye, laying a haze over everything we are trying to see. It also leaves the street in bright pools with dark between them, and eyes that must keep readjusting see less than they would under a steady, even glow. Brightness alone does not make a street safe.

"Won't this be expensive?"

Less than expected. Edison's LS-1 Option E form offers Irvine 2700K, 3000K, or 4000K at the city's discretion, with no price attached to any of the three. Choosing the warmest takes a checkbox and a signature.

The lights already standing will cost something to correct, and we are asking for that anyway. Shields bolt onto fixtures already on the poles. Warmer lamps go in during the maintenance visits Edison is already making, across a service life measured in decades rather than in a single budget year. Irvine chose 4000K when the warm option was offered at no charge, and the price of undoing that choice belongs in the same conversation as the price of making it.

Dear Mayor and Members of the City Council,

I am an Irvine resident writing about the glare from the city's new LED streetlights. These lights reach past the pavement and into our homes.

I am asking the Council to adopt an outdoor lighting standard with three requirements: a color temperature of 2700K or below, full shielding so the light falls on the pavement instead of into windows and yards, and dimming through the late night hours.

Southern California Edison offers Irvine a choice of 2700K, 3000K, or 4000K on these fixtures at the city's discretion, with no price attached to any of the three. The city chose otherwise.

The American Medical Association recommends shielded fixtures at 3000K or below. Irvine's code currently sets no ceiling on brightness or color temperature, which is why this belongs in a written standard rather than in a complaint queue.

I would appreciate a reply telling me when the Council intends to take this up.
