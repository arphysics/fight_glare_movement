Irvine, let's fight glare!

Since 2024, Irvine has been trading warm-hued streetlights for harsh blue-white LEDs. And the city has no lighting ordinance governing glare, brightness, or color. We are a group of concerned residents working to change that.

The Problem

Irvine's new streetlights are too bright, too blue, and too exposed, which leads to

👁️

Visual stress

Intense glare and blue-white light strain the eyes and cast harsh shadows that make it harder, not easier, to see at night.

😴

Sleep and health

Blue-heavy light at night disrupts sleep and circadian rhythms and hits hardest for older adults and residents with migraine, autism, or light sensitivity.

🏠

Light spillage

Unshielded fixtures spill into the sky and into bedrooms and yards, causing unwelcome nuisance in homes and washing out the night sky.

The Goal

Irvine's first lighting ordinance

As of July 2026, Irvine still has no outdoor lighting ordinance in place. Our goal is to help establish a city-wide standard that limits excessive glare and brightness and promotes responsible lighting that is

  • Well-shielded — is aimed downward where it's needed, not into the eyes, windows, or the sky.
  • Warm in tone — is capped at 2700K and no higher, ideally ranging between 1800K and 2400K.
  • Right-sized — is not a whit more than what the task absolutely requires: dimmed or turned off when not actively lighting a pathway or a task.
  • Considerate — is mindful of neighbors and light-sensitive passersby.

Responsible lighting asks that you

Reduce

Use only as much light as you need, and choose warm amber tones over harsh blue-white.

Restrict

Use timers, dimmers, and sensors so light runs only when and where it's actually needed.

Redirect

Shield fixtures to aim light down at the ground — not into eyes, windows, and the sky.

Our three R's distill DarkSky International's Five Principles for Responsible Outdoor Lighting — standards already adopted by cities across the country. Irvine can join them.

The Facts

We're not asking for anything unusual

Warm, shielded lighting is the mainstream recommendation — and cities across California and the country already require it.

The American Medical Association recommends that community street lighting be shielded and no cooler than 3000K to cut glare and protect sleep. We're asking for 2700K. (AMA, 2016)
It's already the standard elsewhere. California's own energy code (Title 24) requires new nonresidential outdoor lighting to be shielded and 3000K or cooler. Communities from Malibu to Los Angeles County to Mono County have dark-sky lighting ordinances — and national leaders like Flagstaff and Tucson have had them for decades. Irvine would simply be catching up.

The two questions people ask

"But isn't brighter safer?"

Not necessarily. Glare creates harsh shadows and blinds aging eyes, which can make streets harder to see. Well-aimed, shielded light improves visibility — good lighting is about quality, not just quantity.

"Won't this be expensive?"

No. Warm, shielded LEDs cost about the same and use just as little energy. An ordinance sets the standard for new and replacement fixtures — it doesn't mean replacing everything overnight.
